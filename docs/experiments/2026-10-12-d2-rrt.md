# D2 RRT family run (Issue #4, prereg v1.0-r3): result, proposed

**Status:** numerical observation, **proposed, not yet reviewed**. This is an instrument check on a null family, not a physics claim. Only the RRT family (`random_recursive_tree`) is reported here. CAY ([note](2026-10-11-d2-cay.md), PR #38) and UST ([note](2026-10-11-d2-ust.md), PR #39) were run and reviewed earlier. ## Budget decision before any RRT output (no r4)

The CAY review left open whether RRT needs a budget revision (r4), because CAY took ≈ 75 CPU-min against the same 50-min §8 projection. Before any RRT invocation, this session ran a **timing-only** comparison (`shell_counts` on one RRT and one CAY instance at N = 2²², non-D2 seed 4242, 12 BFS each, one pinned core; ball counts discarded, no d_eff or verdict computed):

| | generation | per BFS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RRT, 2²² | 1.3 s | 1.23 s |
| CAY, 2²² | 4.0 s | 1.88 s |

The RRT figure matches the 1.2 s/BFS on which §8 based its 50-min projection. CAY is ≈ 1.5× slower per BFS, which accounts for its overrun: `shell_counts` uses pointer jumping, whose number of O(N) passes grows with log₂(depth), and CAY trees are thousands of levels deep while RRTs are ≈ 40 levels deep. **Decision:** the §8 projection (50 min) and the binding mechanical stop (`timeout 75m`, from the CAY review) stand unchanged; no r4. This timing computation is disclosed here in the same way as the §8 r1 timing.

## Gates

- **D2-G4, determinism (RRT part):** `ball_counts.csv` (`4dd7c63c…b030`) and `result.json` (`23d6275f…2091`) are byte-identical between the two invocations; the manifests differ only in runtime and RSS. **PASS (RRT part).** The total output is 2.1 MB, below 5 MiB, so it is committed here (`docs/experiments/2026-10-12-d2-rrt/`).
- **D2-G0, structural (RRT):** all 24 instances have N − 1 edges, are connected and satisfy parent(i) < i (`structural_check` in `result.json`). **PASS.**
- **D2-G3, H-null:** 24/24 replicates return `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE`; none returns `PLATEAU`. **PASS.**

| N | verdicts | admissible scales per replicate | first inadmissible scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2¹⁸ | 8 × `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE` | {4, 8} ×5, {4} ×3 | 16 (×5), 8 (×3) |
| 2²⁰ | 8 × `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE` | {4, 8} ×8 | 16 |
| 2²² | 8 × `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE` | {4, 8} ×8 | 16 |

**Which mechanism decided the null (§4 asks for this):** the **saturation guard**, as §4 predicted heuristically. On the pooled curve B̄(32)/N is 0.985, 0.930 and 0.817 at the three sizes, so r = 16 is never admissible (8·B̄(32) ≈ 6.5–7.9·N > N), leaving at most two admissible scales, fewer than the three the verdict needs.

## Descriptive (not gated)

- **Pooled curve (2048 sources):** `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE` at every size. Pooled d_eff at r = 4 / 8 is 4.00 / 6.00 (2¹⁸), 4.01 / 6.63 (2²⁰), 4.00 / 7.04 (2²²). B̄(16)/N is 0.120, 0.047, 0.016.
- **No plateau even where the guard is not decisive:** d_eff rises with r and, at r = 8, with N (per-replicate ranges at r = 8: 5.80–6.26, 6.51–6.77, 6.86–7.25). This is the volume growth expected for a tree whose typical distances are O(log N) (§4): no finite dyadic ratio. Under the sensitivity setting r_min = 2, where three scales {2, 4, 8} are admissible, 21/24 replicates return `NO_PLATEAU` and 3/24 (all at 2¹⁸) `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE`; so the plateau criterion would also have rejected the null where it could be applied.
- **Sensitivity:** ε ∈ {0.10, 0.20}, r_min = 8 and guard fractions 1/16 and 1/4 give `INSUFFICIENT_RANGE` in all 24 replicates; r_min = 2 is as above. No setting produces a `PLATEAU`, so G3 is not fragile under the mild settings.

## Producer checks (same session as the run; not independent)

- `docs/experiments/d2_rrt_csv_check.py`: recomputes every replicate's B̄(r) from the CSV and calls the frozen D1 `verdict` with the vertex count N. All 24 verdicts agree with `result.json` (0 mismatches); the G3 count agrees with the driver.
- `docs/experiments/d2_rrt_nx_spotcheck.py`:
  - rebuilds two 2¹⁸ instances (reps 0 and 7) from the seed rule, checks with networkx that each is a tree on N vertices, and compares 8 sources each with an independent networkx BFS: **112 of 112** ball counts match;
  - **exact-mathematics check of r = 2:** in any tree, the mean of |B(v, 2)| over a uniform source is 1 + mean(deg²). In an RRT the out-degree of a uniform vertex is asymptotically Geometric(1/2) on {0, 1, …}, so E deg² → 6 and E|B(v, 2)| → 7 (*established result* [Na & Rapoport 1970, "Distribution of nodes of a tree by degree", Math. Biosci. 6, 313–329; to be re-checked by the review], *used as a reference; the finite-N correction is not computed*). The sampled means over 8 × 256 sources are 6.951 ± 0.172, 6.965 ± 0.099 and 6.993 ± 0.104 (± one standard error over the 8 replicates; z = −0.29, −0.35, −0.07). The instance-exact values for the two rebuilt trees are 6.996 and 7.006.

## Interpretation and limitations

- *Numerical observation:* on random recursive trees, which have no finite volume-growth dimension, the frozen D1 estimator never returns `PLATEAU` (0/24). It rejects them through the saturation guard at every size; where three scales can be read (r_min = 2), d_eff grows and the plateau criterion also fails.
- This null tests the **guard**, as §4 and §7 say. It is matched to CAY only in N and in being a tree; it is not a near-miss alternative (e.g. a graph with slowly drifting dimension). Passing G3 says the instrument does not hallucinate a dimension on a small-world tree; it does not bound false plateaus on graphs with a large but finite diameter.
- Same software stack and model family for producer and later reviewers. Single environment, so no cross-environment hash.
