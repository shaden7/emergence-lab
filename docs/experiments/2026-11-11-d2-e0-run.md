## Numerical observation: same total N, different ensembles

D2-E0 always includes two ordered interval endpoints plus N-2 uniform interior
points. D2-M0 instead uses N uniform points and conditions estimates on R>0.
Both prescribe a D=2 diamond; **they are different sampling conventions**, not
the same sample with a post-hoc analysis choice. Compare the same total N.

**Numerical observation:** endpoint E[d(r)]<2 at every tested N=2..32;
the no-endpoint conditional average exceeds 2 at N=4..32. Thus even the
sign of finite-occupancy bias relative to input dimension changes with the
ensemble. No Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty: all combinatorial weights are
exact; numerical discrepancies and finite occupancy are the limitations.

**Model identity:** endpoint E[r]=1/2+(2N-3)/(N(N-1))>1/2. Combined with the
known decreasing MM inversion this explains d(E[r])<2 for finite N. It does
not, by itself, prove the sign of E[d(r)]-2 for arbitrary N: inversion and
averaging do not commute. The latter sign is observed only for N=2..32.
